<Section label="01 · Overview" title="One place for every project">

TM30 PMS is a project management tool built in-house at TM30 Global. It replaces scattered spreadsheets with one place to plan projects, assign work and see where everything stands.

I was a design engineer on the team that built v1. I designed every screen in Figma, built part of the interface in React and contributed to the Django backend.

<Figure
  src="/project/tm30-pms/cover.png"
  alt="TM30 PMS dashboard"
  caption="The TM30 PMS dashboard: status tiles, project list and alerts in one view"
/>

</Section>

<Section label="02 · The problem" title="No one could see the whole picture">

TM30 was running 20+ projects at once, with progress spread across separate spreadsheets and files. For the CEO and management, there was no single place to answer a simple question: how are our projects doing?

<Callout>
  The goal: one tool where leadership sees everything, and teams manage the work.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section label="03 · Research" title="Designing for three kinds of people">

I studied existing project management apps, sketched early ideas, then talked directly to executives, project managers and developers and refined the design with their feedback. Each group needed something different:

<Columns cols={3}>
  <Card eyebrow="Priority 1" title="Executives">
    An easy way to see every project and its key metrics.
  </Card>
  <Card eyebrow="Priority 2" title="Project managers">
    A clear way to manage tasks and people.
  </Card>
  <Card eyebrow="Priority 3" title="Developers & designers">
    One simple place to tick off tasks. Nothing more.
  </Card>
</Columns>

</Section>

<Section label="05 · Outcome" title="Built, adopted and still growing">

TM30 PMS is now in use at TM30, replacing scattered spreadsheets as the place to track projects. Management and teams loved it, and it helped them manage projects better. New screens and features are still being added.

</Section>

<Section label="06 · Reflection" title="What I learned">

- **Act on feedback fast.** Each user group pulled the design in a different direction, and quicker iteration made it better.
- **Learn directly from users.** Conversations with executives, managers and developers shaped the design more than any assumption.
- **Research earlier and deeper.** Next time I would do more research before and during design.

</Section>
